Everyone knows the struggles of riding commercial flights: the tight seats, strict rules, and being forced to interact with average people who aren’t paid to serve you. Luckily, I’m here to share with you my method for fixing this relatable issue: I only fly on private planes, where lack of oversight and incompetence will lead to the fiery death of myself and my entire family.

Here are my top three reasons why I will only fly in a poorly-regulated tin-can destined to be popped like a fourth-of-july firecracker:

1) Cost. This shit costs a lot, and that’s kind of the point. If you got it, you need to flaunt it, and if you aren’t riding precarious vehicles, can you even call yourself a millionaire who won’t be missed? 

Practically speaking, money is the only thing that can secure the privacy of your upscale experience plummeting to the earth at the speed of sound. A charter flight can be anywhere from $1,500 to $20,000. If this seems like a lot to you, that’s perfectly understandable. It’s simply a difference in our philosophies; the elite like myself prefer to use their money on time-saving measures the rest of the population can never hope to afford, like house-cleaners, drivers, and the lightning fast cremation that will take care of me, my wife, and kids as soon as we hit the ground. 

2) Customization. The most annoying part of the modern flying experience is the time it takes to get everything sorted. The bag-checking, the changing gates, and by the end of it, there’s no guarantee you’ll be sitting with someone who came close to sniffing an acceptance to you or your wife’s alma maters. 

While it’s mostly the fault of the knuckle-dragging invalids that take up precious real-estate in the airport on the way to TSA pre-check, it can also be attributed to the dumpster fire that is the Federal Aviation Administration (note: administration is a word that means they’re in charge of stuff). The FAA is the governing body controlling all flights in the United States, and they’re the ones that implement all those pesky rules and regulations around what you can and cannot do on an airplane that you paid to ride. But, as a proud business owner and capitalist, I believe in the free market. I want to buy the ability for my family to throw a few extra hundred pounds of freight on that thing, have the kids jump around the cockpit, everything that makes our half-way flight on our final family vacation exciting and memorable when it flashes in front of our eyes two hours later.

The FAA doesn’t seem to mind. And why should they? It’s a lot easier to handle a lawsuit from one family affected by tragedy than it is the hundreds of families whose loved ones would burn up in a commercial crash.

3) Experience. There are some things money can’t buy, but those are few and far between. As a member of the elite,  I am ever thankful that I have the means to give my family an exclusive once-in-an-end-of-lifetime experience.

Now, speaking of experience, I know what you’re thinking. Or maybe I don’t, because my mind is honed to only think in the ways that someone of my superior standing finds useful and intelligent. But if I were to approximate what you’re thinking right now, I’d turn those useless grunts and moans into a message that, to my people, reads sort of like this: “Where can I find planes like these?” That’s a good question, and the answer is: you can’t. Only myself and those blessed with my resources can. We can’t have everyone with a family crypt that reads: “Gone Too Soon In Private Jet To Aruba.” Stick to a poor-person way to die, like cancer or suicide. Meanwhile, I can rest easy knowing my kids will pass surrounded by people who have never seen the inside of a public school.

I hope I could shed some light on the advantages to orchestrating a hellfire inferno for you and your loved ones. Maybe one day, if you work hard enough, you too can feel the pressure of 9 Gs crushing you and your kin before you become a crater in the middle of Alabama.
